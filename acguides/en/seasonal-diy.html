Seasonal DIY materials: a year at a glance

Cherry blossoms, bamboo, shells, acorns and pine cones, mushrooms, maple leaves, snowflakes, ornaments: when to gather them and what to make

Some DIY materials only show up during set windows each year. Cherry-blossom petals and maple leaves fall for just 10 days, and snowflakes and ornaments only come around in winter. Once the window closes, you won't find them in the trees, on the beach, or in the air, and you'll have to wait until next year to craft the furniture that uses them.

In the Northern Hemisphere, fall and winter are the busiest stretch: from September, shaking trees drops acorns and pine cones; mushrooms grow by trees all of November, maple leaves squeeze in from November 16–25, and snowflakes start falling on December 11. So look over the yearly table below first and note which days you need to log in and gather. Most DIY recipes come from shooting down balloons, the frozen series comes from snowboys, and if you miss a season entirely, you can still try to catch up on Kapp'n's boat.

01The yearly table: when and how to gather

All dates in the table are in-game dates. The Southern Hemisphere's seasons are exactly half a year off from the north's, so most materials shift by 6 months. The only exception is ornaments, which follow the end of the year: December 15 to January 6 in both hemispheres.

None of these are hard to get: young spring bamboo comes from hitting bamboo with an axe, summer shells are just lying on the beach, and acorns and pine cones drop when you shake trees (pine cones only grow on cedars). Cherry-blossom petals, maple leaves, and snowflakes all float through the air, so you'll need to swing your net to catch them. Ornaments also drop when you shake cedar trees.

Cherry blossoms and maple leaves only last 10 days, so they're the easiest to miss. Snowflakes stick around for over two months, but with those two, if you don't log in much during those days, that's it for the year.

Fall and winter are the busiest

In the Northern Hemisphere, acorns and pine cones start dropping in September and keep going until December 10. Mushrooms grow by trees all of November, and maple leaves cut in from November 16–25. Snowflakes start falling on December 11, and from December 15 to January 6, cedars are hung with ornaments. With all these materials arriving back to back, it's worth doing a lap of your island every few days during these months. Spring bamboo and cherry blossoms have to wait until the following spring.

In the Southern Hemisphere, everything shifts by half a year: cherry blossoms fall from October 1–10, spring bamboo lasts until November 30, and summer shells start washing up on December 1. Ornaments start on December 15, the same as in the north.

02Where the DIY recipes come from

Having the materials isn't enough — you need to learn the matching DIY recipes before you can craft anything. There are three main sources.

The big one is shooting down balloons. During a season, balloons floating overhead drop that season's DIY recipes, and they'll keep doing so until you've learned them all. When you spot a balloon during a material season, chase it down and pop it.

Next is visiting Resident Services on the first day of a season. Tom Nook or Isabelle will give you one recipe for the season, like the bamboo noodle slide for spring bamboo season, the outdoor picnic set for cherry-blossom season, or the mushroom wreath for mushroom season. You only get these on the first day of the season, so pop into Resident Services first thing when you log in.

The third is Kapp'n's boat tours: on some seasonal islands, message bottles contain that season's DIY recipes. More on that in section 05.

Of the 26 snowflake recipes, 16 (the frozen series, like the frozen bed) only come from snowboys — balloons never drop them. To learn the whole frozen series, you'll need to build snowboy after snowboy on snowy days.

TipAll of these materials can go in storage. Stock up near the end of a season so you can keep crafting after it's over. And if you haven't learned every recipe for the season yet, no worries — you can pull the materials back out once you do.

03Building a perfect snowboy

Snowballs only appear from December 11 to February 24 in the Northern Hemisphere and June 11 to August 24 in the Southern — the same window as snowflakes. They won't spawn while your island's gates are open, so build snowboys when your gates are closed.

Find two snowballs and push them along the ground; they'll get bigger as they roll. Then push one into the other to stack them into a snowboy. For a perfect snowboy, both need to be big, with the head just a bit smaller than the body. Don't rush to stack them — roll each one up to size first.

A perfect snowboy gives you a large snowflake and one frozen-series DIY recipe. Those 16 frozen recipes can only be obtained this way, so it's worth building plenty of snowboys during snowy season.

Your very first snowboy is more forgiving and will reward you no matter how it turns out. A snowboy lasts 4 days on your island, and if you chat with him on each of the next 3 days, he'll give you another large snowflake each time. Once you've learned every frozen-series recipe, perfect snowboys only give large snowflakes.

04Gathering mushrooms

During mushroom season, four kinds pop up next to trees every day: round, skinny, flat, and elegant mushrooms. Walk past all the trees on your island and you'll pick up the full set.

Rare mushrooms are a little better hidden: they're buried in the dirt next to trees, and you have to dig them up with a shovel. A rare mushroom sells for 16,000 Bells, making it the most valuable of all these seasonal materials, so selling any you don't need for crafting is no loss.

You can also eat mushrooms. They give you a burst of strength, enough to dig up an entire tree and move it somewhere else.

05Missed a season? Catch up on Kapp'n's boat

Kapp'n at the pier will take you out to sea once a day for 1,000 Nook Miles. Every so often, he'll drop you on an island stuck in a particular season: there's one each for cherry blossoms, bamboo, summer shells, mushrooms, maple leaves, and winter, each with about a 3% chance. On that island, you can gather that season's materials, and message bottles on the beach may hold seasonal DIY recipes.

The odds are low, so this is really just luck. There's also a catch: your own island has to have gone through that season at least once before you can be taken to the matching island.

Once you've decorated your hotel's first VIP room, Kapp'n will take you on an extra trip each day for 100 hotel tickets. Two trips a day means a slightly better shot at a seasonal island.
