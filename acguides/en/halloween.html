Halloween: starts 5 PM on October 31, so stock up on candy early

Jack, candy, lollipops, and the spooky DIY recipes

Halloween happens just one night a year: October 31 from 5 PM to midnight, on the same day in both hemispheres. That night your villagers wander the island in costume asking everyone for candy, and Jack roams around Resident Services. The spooky carriage, Jack's face, and Jack's robe all have to be collected that night.

The only thing you really need to prep ahead is candy. Nook's Cranny only sells 1 piece of candy per player per day, so if you wait until the 31st, you'll only be able to buy one. Below we'll cover what you can do during October first, then how to spend your candy on the night.

01What to do during October

On October 1, Jack sends you a letter with Jack's portrait enclosed, as an early hello.

Buy one candy a day

Also starting in October, Nook's Cranny stocks candy at 120 Bells each. It's cheap — the catch is that each player can only buy 1 per day. Make it a habit to grab one every time you log on, starting at the beginning of the month, and you'll have about thirty by the end.

If you have a second character on your island, send them to the shop every day too. When visiting a friend's island, pick one up at their Nook's Cranny as well. That way you can get an extra one or two per day.

Grow pumpkins

Nearly all spooky furniture needs pumpkins, and orange pumpkins are used the most — the spooky carriage alone takes 30 (see the materials table in section 04). In October, Nook's Cranny stocks pumpkin starts for 280 Bells each, and Leif carries them year-round. If you're planning to make the full set, get your pumpkins planted early.

Learn the spooky DIY recipes

Balloons you pop in October can drop spooky DIY recipes. If you catch a villager crafting at home, they may teach you one too. If you already know the recipe they're working on, they'll give you something else instead: 70% of the time it's 5 orange pumpkins, 30% of the time it's 1 piece of candy.

Costumes and makeup

This month Able Sisters stocks Halloween costumes like the raggedy outfit. Visiting villagers' homes in a Halloween costume on the night earns you candy (more in section 03), so get at least one. To paint your face too, redeem Nook Miles for Body-Paint Costume Tips and Exploring New Eye Colors, 2,400 miles each.

On October 28, two spooky towers go up in front of Resident Services, and Tom Nook, Isabelle, and the others start wearing impish horns and mage's striped hats. Once you see these, Halloween is just a few days away.

02The night of the 31st

After 5 PM, villagers out and about are all in costume. Talk to them and they'll ask you for candy. You have three ways to respond.

Give candy, and the villager gives you a spooky furniture item in return. The first few rewards come in a fixed order: first the spooky garland or spooky table setting, then the spooky rug, then the spooky flooring or spooky wall, then the DIY recipes for the garland and table setting, and finally any other spooky DIY recipes you haven't learned yet. Once you have all of those, further candy gets you random furniture, DIY recipes, or pumpkins.

Give a lollipop, and the villager always gives you a spooky candy set. But lollipops are hard to come by, so it's usually best to save them for Jack — here's why.

Give nothing, and the villager plays a trick on you, smearing paint all over your face.

Trick or treat: no candy means a prank (Image: Nookipedia)

Where lollipops come from

The shop doesn't sell lollipops — the only way to get them is trading candy with villagers. When a villager accepts candy, besides giving you furniture, there's a chance they'll slip you a lollipop too. The chance goes up with the number of candies you've already given, and by the 4th candy it's guaranteed. Once you get one, the count resets.

Jack

Jack spends the night near Resident Services. He'll ask you for candy too, but what you get back depends a lot on whether you give him candy or a lollipop.

Give him a lollipop, and your first three get you the spooky carriage, the spooky carriage DIY recipe, and the spooky wand DIY recipe, in that order. From the fourth on, he gives you Jack's face or Jack's robe.

Give him candy, and there's a 30% chance of getting the face or robe; otherwise he just hands you some pumpkins.

The carriage and both of those DIY recipes can only come from Jack, so give him your first three lollipops. Use any extras for the face and robe, or give them to villagers for spooky candy sets.

03Running low on candy that night?

If you didn't stock up enough candy, there are two ways to make up for it on the night.

One is to visit villagers' homes in a Halloween costume. A villager at home gives you a piece of candy every 5 minutes, so hang around inside for a while to collect a few more.

The other is to wear Jack's face and Jack's robe together. Villagers will mistake you for Jack and give you candy instead — though each villager only falls for it once. While you're dressed like this, villagers outside won't chase you down for candy either.

04How many pumpkins spooky furniture takes

Here are the main materials for common spooky items. The carriage takes the most: besides 30 orange pumpkins, it needs 20 each of the three kinds of wood plus 10 iron nuggets.

DIY recipeMain materials
spooky lantern4 orange pumpkins
spooky tower7 orange pumpkins
spooky table14 orange pumpkins, 10 softwood
spooky candy set1 orange pumpkin, 3 candy
spooky cookies1 flour, 1 pumpkin of each of the four colors
spooky carriage30 orange pumpkins, 20 each of the three woods, 10 iron nuggets
spooky wand1 spooky lantern, 3 star fragments

Spooky furniture can be customized after crafting: use pumpkins of different colors to switch it to orange, yellow, green, or white. If you want colors other than orange, save a few pumpkins of the other colors too.

TipEach spooky candy set takes 3 candy. On the night of the 31st, put your candy toward lollipops first, and craft candy sets after the event is over.

05Version changes

Halloween was added in the Ver. 1.5.0 fall update. Since Ver. 2.0, seasonal events arrive automatically every year with no update needed. The Ver. 3.0 patch notes don't mention Halloween, so everything works the same as before.
