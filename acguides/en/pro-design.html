Pro Designs: turn photos into clothing and wall art

Pick a type, import a photo, touch it up, generate QR codes, and scan them into the game

Painting a shirt pixel by pixel in the game takes forever. Nook Circle's Pro Designs turns photos on your phone straight into in-game designs: tidy up the details, generate QR codes, scan them in with the Nintendo Switch App, and you can wear them, hang them on the wall, or lay them on the ground.

Two things to know before you start. First, clothing and hats can only be made in the iOS app; Nook Circle's WeChat mini program only makes 32×32 patterns, which are enough for wall art, floor tiles, and flags. Second, getting designs into the game relies on the Nintendo Switch App, which requires a Nintendo Switch Online membership. Without one, you can make the codes but you won't be able to scan them in.

01Decide what you're making first

In the app, you'll find Pro Designs on the Handbook tab; in the mini program, it's My Design on the home page. If you spot something you like while browsing the Handbook, you can also tap the QR button on its detail page (on villager pages it's called Make Sign) to start a design using that item's image as the base.

The app starts on Pattern by default. To make clothing, tap the type button in the toolbar and pick a style before you start. Here are the sizes for each type and how many codes each one generates:

TypeSizeQR codes
Pattern32×321
Shirt / dress (long-sleeve, short-sleeve, sleeveless)64×644
Knit cap / horned hat32×321
Standee64×644

Clothing and hats both have a 3D preview, so you can spin them around as you draw. Gray areas on a clothing canvas are cut away by that style — anything you paint there won't show up.

NoteSwitching types clears the canvas. Always pick your type before you start drawing.

02Import a photo and pick the right quality

Tap Import and choose an image from your photos. The mini program reminds you to send the original — be sure to tick "Original" when choosing, or the image gets compressed first and comes out blurrier. On the crop screen, drag and pinch to frame the part you want.

Choosing a quality setting

This step affects the result more than anything else. Choose based on what the image looks like.

For regular photos, choose Photo (recommended) in the app. The mini program has two photo options, one for noisy photos and one for sharp photos; results vary by image, so try both and pick the better one.

For images that are already pixel art, choose Pixel art. If the image has been scaled up and the grid doesn't line up, use "Pixel art, auto-aligned" in the app — it tells you what grid size it detected.

For images with very few colors to begin with, use Basic resize. It just shrinks the image and does nothing else.

Other settings — only if the colors look off

Once you've picked a quality setting, most images are good to go. If the colors look wrong, come back and adjust these:

· Colour picking defaults to Standard. If the colors are off, try Fine clustering or Split by blocks.

· Alpha cutoff is for PNGs with the background already removed.

· Vividness (app): turn it up to Strong if the colors look washed out.

· Dither (mini program): you can turn on light dithering for photos, but leave it off for icons and text.

· Blank fill (app) decides what color fills the transparent areas on clothing, hats, and standees. The default is white.

Covering a whole wall

A single pattern is only 32×32, which looks small as a big painting. When making a pattern, you can split one image into tiles — up to 15 across and 15 down, 49 tiles in total — and generate all the QR codes in one go. In the game, lay the tiles on the ground or hang them on the wall by number, and the full picture comes back together.

Changing just part of an outfit (app)

When making clothing, you get an extra Import into option that places the photo only on the front, back, sleeves, or skirt. For example, give the whole outfit a base color first, then put an image just on the front.

03Touch up the details

After converting a photo, small areas like edges and eyes often turn into a blur, so you'll want to fix them by hand.

The pen paints one pixel at a time, and the paint bucket fills a whole connected area of the same color. If you slip up, just undo. In the app you can also shift the whole image around and zoom the canvas to 2×, 4×, or 8×, which makes small fixes much easier.

There's one hard limit on colors: each design can use up to 15 colors, and the 16th slot is transparent. Colors can only come from the game's built-in palette. Change a color in the palette and every pixel using that slot changes with it — handy for adjusting the overall colors.

Edit the design title, designer, and island name under Design details; they're printed on the QR code card.

Found a design code someone else shared? Tap Scan to bring it in and keep editing from there. The app can scan with the camera or read a screenshot from your photos. You don't have to scan the 4 codes for clothing in order — the screen tells you which ones are still missing. The mini program can only scan single-code patterns.

04Generate QR codes and scan them into the game

When you're done, tap Generate. For clothing, the app asks whether you want "Separate images (4)" or "One combined image." To scan into the game, choose separate images; the combined image is for sharing with friends. Tap Save all to save the codes to your photos.

The app keeps your generated designs in Pro Design History — up to 50 of them — so you can pull one up and keep editing later.

Scanning into the game

1. Sign up for Nintendo Switch Online first. If it's your first time, press − on the game's title screen to turn on NookLink.

2. On your phone, open Nintendo's official Nintendo Switch App and sign in with the same account as your Switch. Under game-specific services, choose Animal Crossing: New Horizons, go to Designs, and select the QR code scanner.

After scanning the codes for an outfit, the Nintendo Switch App asks whether to save it (Japanese version shown) (Image: Nintendo)

3. Open the saved QR codes on another phone, tablet, or computer and scan them off the screen. Scan the 4 codes for clothing in one go, in the 1→2→3→4 order marked on the cards.

4. Back in the game, open the Custom Designs app on your NookPhone, press − to download, and save the design to an empty slot.

TipMenu names in the Nintendo Switch App and the game may vary a little between versions. Just look for words like "Designs" and "Download."

05Common questions

Transparent areas on clothing turned into color streaks

Only patterns support true transparency. On clothing, any area left transparent gets filled by the game dragging a neighboring color across it. So don't leave transparency on clothing — fill every blank area with a color (in the app, Blank fill does it all at once).

The colors look wrong

A photo has thousands of colors and 15 can never hold them all, so some color shift is unavoidable. Try a few combinations of quality and Colour picking settings, pick the closest one, then fix the key colors in the palette.

I want it to look more like the game

Below the design screen there's an AI image prompt. Copy it into an AI tool like ChatGPT or Gemini and have it redraw your photo as 32×32 pixel art first. Then import that image back with the Pixel art quality setting — the result comes out much cleaner.

The QR code won't scan

If an image has been compressed too heavily, the QR code may not scan. When sending codes to friends, remember to send the original image.

Can't make clothing in the mini program

Nook Circle's WeChat mini program and the iOS app share the same save once you sign in with WeChat. To make clothing, just sign in to the iOS app with the same WeChat account.
