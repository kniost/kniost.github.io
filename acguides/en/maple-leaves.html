Maple leaf season: November 16 to 25, catch leaves with your net

Only 10 days a year: how to catch maple leaves, where the DIY recipes come from, and a materials table for all 10 items

Like cherry-blossom season, maple leaf season only lasts 10 days a year: November 16 to 25 in the Northern Hemisphere and May 16 to 25 in the Southern Hemisphere. During these days, red maple leaves drift through the air, and you can swing your net to catch them.

Maple leaves go into 10 DIY recipes: the red-leaf pile, maple-leaf umbrella, maple-leaf pond stone, tree's bounty big tree, and more. Crafting all of them takes 57 maple leaves. Two of them also need acorns and pine cones, but luckily acorn and pine cone season hasn't ended yet, so you can collect them together.

01Which 10 days

All dates are in-game dates. The Southern Hemisphere runs half a year behind the north.

HemisphereStartsEnds
NorthernNovember 16November 25
SouthernMay 16May 25

An in-game day starts at 5 AM, so late on the night of the 10th day, right up until 5 AM the next morning, still counts as maple leaf season.

These 10 days fall within mushroom season (November in the north, May in the south) and acorn and pine cone season, so you can gather all three materials together. In some years, Turkey Day on the fourth Thursday of November also lands within these 10 days.

02How to catch maple leaves

Maple leaves float through the air. It's just like catching bugs: aim your net and swing. Each leaf sells for only 200 Bells, so it's not worth selling; keep them for furniture.

Maple leaves only stack up to 10 per pocket slot, so your pockets fill up quickly. Put them in storage every so often to free up space and keep catching.

TipMaple leaves can go in storage. Stock up near the end of the season so you can keep crafting after it's over. And if you haven't learned every recipe for the season yet, no worries — you can pull them back out once you do.

03Where the DIY recipes come from

With 10 recipes to learn in 10 days, time is tight, so you'll want to use all three sources.

The big one is shooting down balloons. During maple leaf season, balloons floating overhead drop this season's DIY recipes, and they'll keep doing so until you've learned them all. For these few days, chase every balloon you see.

Next is visiting Resident Services on the first day. On November 16 in the north or May 16 in the south, Tom Nook or Isabelle will give you the red-leaf pile. You only get it on the first day.

The third is Kapp'n's boat tours, covered below.

Missed it? Catch up on Kapp'n's boat

Kapp'n at the pier will take you out to sea once a day for 1,000 Nook Miles. Every so often, he'll drop you on an island stuck in a particular season, and the maple leaf island is one of them; each seasonal island has about a 3% chance. On that island you can catch maple leaves and shake down acorns, and message bottles on the beach may hold maple leaf DIY recipes.

The odds are low, so this is really just luck. There's also a catch: your own island has to have gone through this season at least once before you can be taken to the matching island. Once you've decorated your hotel's first VIP room, Kapp'n will take you on an extra trip each day (for 100 hotel tickets), which gives you a slightly better shot.

Crafting all 10 takes 57 maple leaves in total, plus 24 weeds, 23 tree branches, 10 stones, 9 acorns, 9 pine cones, 8 wood, and 4 clay.

TipThe acorns and pine cones for the tree's bounty arch and tree's bounty big tree have to be collected by shaking trees (hardwood trees for acorns, cedar trees for pine cones). Acorn and pine cone season lasts until December 10 in the Northern Hemisphere and June 10 in the Southern, so you can keep shaking after maple leaf season ends.

05What to make first

Red-leaf pile: the one you get on the first day. A pile of red leaves on the ground, and it takes just 3 maple leaves.

Autumn wall + colored-leaves flooring: turn your room into a red-leaf forest. They take 10 maple leaves each, the most of anything this season.

Tree's bounty arch: a 3-tile-wide arch hung with nuts, cones, and maple leaves. It looks great at a path entrance and can be customized in different colors.

Maple-leaf umbrella, maple-leaf pochette: small things to carry or wear, perfect for fall photos.

Maple-leaf pond stone: a stone basin with maple leaves floating in it. It takes 10 stones and fits right into a Japanese garden.
