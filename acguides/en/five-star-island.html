How to get a 5-star island rating

How Isabelle scores your island, what to check when you're stuck at 4 stars, and a checklist to finish

Reach a 5-star island rating and Isabelle gives you the golden watering can DIY recipe, and lilies of the valley start popping up on their own. Lots of players cruise up to 4 stars and then get stuck: tons of furniture out, plenty of flowers planted, and still 4 stars every time they ask.

Usually it comes down to two things. First, the score is actually split into two parts, and pushing only one of them won't get you there. Second, some things on your island are quietly costing you points, like items lying on the ground, weeds, too many trees, and clusters of identical furniture. Below, we'll cover how Isabelle scores you, where to look when you're stuck at 4 stars, and finish with a 5-star checklist.

01Ask Isabelle for your rating

Once Resident Services becomes a proper building, Isabelle takes her place at the counter. Talk to her and ask about your island's rating, and she'll tell you how many stars you have.

Your rating is calculated the moment you ask — no need to wait until the next day. So the easiest approach is to ask again each time you finish working on an area; you'll know right away whether the change helped.

If your score isn't high enough yet, she'll point out what's lacking, like too few flowers or too much trash. Following her hints is much faster than guessing on your own.

Inside Resident Services — that's Isabelle behind the counter on the right

On the road to 5 stars, 3 stars is the first big milestone. Once you reach it, K.K. Slider comes to play a concert and you get the Island Designer app, which lets you dig rivers, build cliffs, and lay paths — pretty much everything you'll do to remodel your island later depends on it.

02How the score works

The game splits your score into two parts: development points cover the "popularity" side — facilities, residents, and furniture — while scenery points cover the "nature" side — flowers, plants, and trees. Each star requires both to clear their thresholds. No matter how high one is, if the other falls short, you won't move up.

Where development points come from

Facilities and residents form the base. The museum and Able Sisters add 15 points each, and a full 10 residents scores noticeably more than 9. Every bridge and incline also adds 15 points, so four or five of them add up to a nice chunk.

The real bulk comes from outdoor furniture. Every item placed outside counts, pricier items score higher, and large items 3 or more tiles wide get a bonus. Each section of fencing adds only a tiny bit, but lots of it adds up.

Where scenery points come from

Each fully grown tree or bamboo counts as 1 point, and each bush as half a point. Flowers score at every stage from seed to bloom, with the most points when they're blooming. Outdoor DIY furniture also adds a little to your scenery score.

03Stuck at 4 stars? Check these four things

If you've got plenty of furniture and flowers but your rating won't budge, something is probably costing you points. Go through these four one by one.

Clean up the ground

Fruit, branches, and shells dropped on the ground, plus empty cans, boots, and old tires you fished up and left lying around — Isabelle sees all of it as trash. Neatly placed outdoor furniture doesn't count, so place what you want displayed and pocket or sell the rest.

Fruit, branches, and weeds scattered everywhere — all of it drags down your rating (Image: TechBang)

Pull the weeds

Lots of weeds directly cut into your development points. If pulling them yourself is too much work, Leif at the garden shop can weed your island for just 60,000 Bells, as long as there are at least 30 weeds.

Don't plant too many trees

Trees add scenery points, but overdoing it backfires. With more than about 220 trees (bamboo included), Isabelle says the island is overgrown and you can't get 5 stars. To be safe, keep your island at 100–200 trees.

Don't cluster identical furniture

The game divides your island into 8×8 blocks to score furniture. Line up a row of identical items in the same block, and the extras not only add nothing but actually cost you points. If you want lots of the same item, spread them out across the island.

NoteTree and flower points can make up for each other. If your scenery score is short, plant more flowers instead of adding more trees.

04Your 5-star checklist

Here's roughly what most players' islands look like when they hit 5 stars. These are rules of thumb, not hard requirements, but if you tick them off one by one, you usually won't be far off.

1. A full 10 residents, with the museum and Able Sisters both built.

2. At least 4 bridges and inclines combined.

3. Two to three hundred pieces of outdoor furniture, as varied as possible and spread across the island.

4. Two to three hundred flowers, with trees kept at 100–200.

5. Some fencing — around 100 sections is plenty.

6. All weeds pulled and everything on the ground picked up.

7. Ask Isabelle after finishing each area, and shore up whatever she points out.

Stone paths, fences, flower beds, and outdoor furniture — it all adds points

TipTwo to three hundred pieces of furniture sounds daunting, but you don't need to spend a fortune. Things you craft yourself, prizes from the Fishing Tourney and Bug-Off, and gifts from popped balloons all count just the same once they're out.

05After you reach 5 stars

Golden watering can

Once you hit 5 stars, Isabelle gives you the golden watering can DIY recipe, which takes a watering can plus a gold nugget. The golden watering can waters a 3×3 area in front of you — 9 tiles at once — making big flower fields much faster to water. Use it on black roses, and the next new flowers that grow will be gold roses.

Lilies of the valley

Lilies of the valley only grow naturally on 5-star islands. Whether and where they appear is pure luck, and they often pop up on top of cliffs. You can't pick them, so to move one you have to dig up the whole plant with a shovel and replant it. Flowers can go into storage too, so if you find one you like, dig it up and store it.

Lilies of the valley on a cliff edge after reaching 5 stars — you can't pick them, only dig up the whole plant (Image: TechBang)

NoteMajor remodeling after reaching 5 stars can drop you back to 4, and no new lilies of the valley will grow while you're down a star. Keep that in mind before starting a big project.

06Common misconceptions

Interior decorating doesn't count. Your island rating only looks at the outdoors. How you decorate inside affects your Happy Home Academy score, not your island rating.

More furniture isn't always better. You do need enough, but duplicates in the same block cost you points, so spreading things out beats piling them up.

More trees aren't always better, either. Go over the cap and 5 stars is off the table. If your scenery score is short, planting more flowers is the safer bet.

No need to wait a day after changes. Your rating is calculated when you ask, so you can check right after making changes.

07Version changes

Leif's weeding service has been around since Ver. 2.0; Ver. 3.0 cut the price to 60,000 Bells and lets you hire him once your island has 30 or more weeds. Putting flowers into storage was added in Ver. 3.0.
