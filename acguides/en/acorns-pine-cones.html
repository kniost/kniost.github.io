Acorn and pine cone season: September to early December, shake your trees

Which trees to shake, where the DIY recipes come from, a materials table for all 10 tree's bounty items, and why to save some for maple leaf season

Once fall arrives, shaking the trees on your island can drop acorns and pine cones. In the Northern Hemisphere the season runs from September 1 to December 10, and in the Southern Hemisphere from March 1 to June 10. That's over three months, so there's plenty of time.

These two go into 10 DIY recipes: the tree's bounty little tree, tree's bounty lamp, leaf campfire, acorn rug, and more. On top of that, two maple leaf season recipes (the tree's bounty arch and tree's bounty big tree) also use them. Counting those two, you'll need 41 acorns and 31 pine cones in total.

In the Northern Hemisphere, the second half of this season overlaps with mushroom season (all of November) and maple leaf season (November 16–25); in the Southern Hemisphere, the same thing happens in May. That's when your island has the most materials to gather, so do a lap every few days.

02How to shake for them

Walk up to a tree and give it a shake, and sometimes something will fall out. Acorns drop from hardwood trees and pine cones drop from cedar trees, so you'll need to shake both kinds to get both.

Most of what falls is tree branches; acorns and pine cones are much rarer, so it's a matter of shaking tree after tree and hoping for luck. Both sell for 200 Bells and stack up to 30 per pocket slot.

NoteShaking trees can also knock down a wasp nest, and the wasps will chase you and sting. Have your net in hand before you shake so you can catch them in time.

TipAcorns and pine cones can go in storage. Stock up near the end of the season so you can keep crafting after it's over. And if you haven't learned every recipe for the season yet, no worries — you can pull them back out once you do.

03Where the DIY recipes come from

The big one is shooting down balloons. During this season, balloons floating overhead drop this season's DIY recipes, and they'll keep doing so until you've learned them all.

Next is visiting Resident Services on the first day of the season. On September 1 in the north or March 1 in the south, Tom Nook or Isabelle will give you the tree's bounty little tree. You only get it on the first day.

Kapp'n's boat tours don't have a dedicated acorn and pine cone island, but you can shake acorns and pine cones from the trees on the mushroom island, and acorns on the maple leaf island. Message bottles on the maple leaf island's beach may also hold this season's DIY recipes. Each seasonal island has about a 3% chance, so this is really just luck.

Save some for the two maple leaf recipes

The two DIY recipes below belong to maple leaf season, but their materials include acorns and pine cones. In the Northern Hemisphere, maple leaf season runs November 16–25, while you can still shake down acorns and pine cones, so you can craft as you go.

DIY recipeMaterials
tree's bounty arch4 pine cones, 5 acorns, 5 maple leaves, 15 tree branches
tree's bounty big tree5 pine cones, 4 acorns, 4 maple leaves, 8 tree branches, 4 clay

Together, these two take 9 acorns and 9 pine cones. Set them aside first instead of using everything on other items.

05What to make first

Tree's bounty little tree: the one you get on the first day. A little tree hung with nuts and cones, and it can be customized in different colors.

Tree's bounty lamp, leaf campfire: both look very autumnal outdoors, and both can be customized.

Pile of leaves, yellow-leaf pile: piles of fallen leaves you can lay right on the ground as decoration. They only take pine cones or acorns plus weeds, so they're very cheap to make.

Acorn rug, acorn pochette: 6 acorns each. One goes on the floor, the other on your back.
