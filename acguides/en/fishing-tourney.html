Fishing Tourney: dates, scoring, and prizes

C.J. comes once a season — three-minute rounds where only the number of fish counts

The Fishing Tourney happens four times a year, on the second Saturday of January, April, July, and October, on the same day in both hemispheres. On tourney day, C.J. sets up a tent in the plaza. Each round lasts three minutes, and every fish you catch is worth 1 point, no matter how big or rare it is. Save up points to trade with him for fishing-themed furniture and clothing, and once your total hits 100, 200, and 300, he'll mail you bronze, silver, and gold trophies.

To rack up points, stock up on fish bait ahead of time, and bring a spare fishing rod if you can. It's even better if you can get friends from other islands to join: entry is free, and everyone gets 5 bonus points per round. Below, we'll go through the day in order: where to sign up, how scoring works, how to team up with friends, how to fish faster, and finally the prizes.

01When and where

The Fishing Tourney is always on the second Saturday of January, April, July, and October, the same day in both hemispheres. In-game, they're named by season: the Winter, Spring, Summer, and Fall Fishing Tourneys. About a week before, a notice goes up on the bulletin board next to Resident Services — that's your cue to start making fish bait.

On the day, the host, C.J., sets up a tent in the plaza in front of Resident Services. Sign-ups open at 9 AM and close at 6 PM. He doesn't leave right away after that, though — he sticks around until 8 PM, so you can still sell him fish and redeem prizes.

02Signing up and scoring

Talk to C.J. and tell him you want to enter. Your first round of the day is free; after that, each round costs 500 Bells. You can keep signing up until 6 PM, with no limit — as long as you have the Bells, play as many rounds as you like.

Each round is 3 minutes. Fish you catch go straight into the cooler box under the tent instead of your pockets, so a full inventory is fine. All you need is a fishing rod in hand.

Scoring is simple: 1 point per fish — a sea bass is worth the same as a coelacanth. Catch 3 or more in a round and you get 2 bonus points. In other words, it's all about how many fish you can land in three minutes; size and rarity don't matter, so quick hands beat luck.

After each round, you have to empty the cooler box before starting the next one. You can either sell the whole box to C.J. or take out the fish you want to keep. C.J. pays 1.5 times what Nook's Cranny does, so selling him any fish you don't plan to keep is the best deal.

During a round, the top of the screen shows the time left and how many fish you've caught (Image: Nookipedia)

03Fishing with friends

When visitors from other islands join in, C.J. waives the entry fee — every round is free. Everyone's timer starts at the same moment and won't wait for anyone, so have everybody stand at the water's edge before signing up instead of running to the beach after the clock starts.

On top of individual scores, if the whole group catches 5 fish combined in a round, everyone gets 5 bonus points, no matter how many you caught yourself. With a few people fishing, 5 is easy to hit, so that's basically a free 5 points every round.

Heads upThe next round can't start until everyone's cooler box is empty. After each round, have everybody go back to the tent and sell or take their fish before signing up together again.

04How to fish faster

Three minutes goes fast, and most of it gets eaten up looking for fish shadows. The key to saving time is not running all over the island hunting for them.

Make fish bait ahead of time. Dig up manila clams from the little spurting holes on the beach, then craft them into fish bait. Toss some into the water and a shadow appears almost right away, so you can keep casting without moving. For a three-minute round, having 10 or more on hand is a safe bet.

Find a straight stretch of coastline. The ocean has plenty of fish shadows, and casting from a long, straight beach is the smoothest. Rivers twist and turn and the pier is cramped, so shadows often show up where it's awkward to cast.

Catch everything you see. Small fish are 1 point too. Don't skip tiny shadows — being picky just wastes time.

Bring a spare fishing rod. Your rod won't break mid-tourney, but its durability still ticks down, so it might snap right as a round ends. Having a backup means you won't have to craft one on the spot before the next round.

TipIf there are fish you want to keep — ones you haven't donated to the museum yet, or ones missing from your Critterpedia — take them out of the cooler box before selling, or they'll go with the rest of the box.

05Prizes and trophies

Every 10 points you save up can be traded to C.J. for one prize. There are 13 prizes in total, given at random, and you won't get duplicates until you've received all 13 once. Any points you don't spend carry over to the next tourney.

The 13 prizes are: fishing-rod stand, fresh cooler, tackle bag, fish pochette, fish-print tee, fish umbrella, fish doorplate, fish rug, marine pop wall, fish print, fish-drying rack, anchor statue, and fish wand.

The fish wand is a magic wand: give it a wave and you'll instantly change into a full outfit saved in your wardrobe.

Trophies don't cost points. When your lifetime total reaches 100, 200, and 300, C.J. will mail you the bronze fish trophy, silver fish trophy, and gold fish trophy. Points spent on prizes and points from earlier tourneys all count toward that total, so redeeming prizes won't slow down your trophies.

Left to right: bronze fish trophy, silver fish trophy, gold fish trophy (Image: Nookipedia)

TipSolo, you'll catch around 10 fish a round; add the 2-point bonus for 3+ fish and that's about 12 points, so reaching 300 takes twenty-some rounds. With friends you get 5 extra points per round and no entry fee, which speeds things up a lot.
