Getting started: your first week on the island

The main story day by day, plus the little daily chores and early ways to make Bells

Animal Crossing runs on real time, so a lot of things on your island mean "wait until tomorrow": hand over the materials and the shop opens the next day; donate enough creatures and Blathers sets up his tent the next day. It's normal not to finish everything in one sitting during your first few days. No need to stay up late grinding — just follow the order below and make a bit of progress each day.

Your first week roughly goes like this: pay off your tent with Nook Miles, bring Blathers to the island, build your house, open the shop, get homes ready for new residents, and finally push your island rating to 3 stars. 3 stars is your first big goal. Once you hit it, K.K. Slider comes to play a concert, and you'll get the Island Designer app for terraforming. That said, few players reach 3 stars in the first week — two or three weeks is normal, so don't rush.

01Day 1: Pitch your tent, earn Nook Miles

When you arrive, pick a spot for your tent, then follow Tom Nook around the island picking up branches and fruit to get the hang of the controls. That evening everyone gathers around the campfire for a party, and this is when you name your island.

The first-night campfire party, with Tom Nook addressing everyone (Image: Nintendo)

After the party, Tom Nook explains Nook Miles and DIY. Your tent costs 5,000 Nook Miles — no Bells needed. Open the Nook Miles+ app on your NookPhone for a list of small tasks like fishing, catching bugs, shaking trees, and crafting. Each time you finish one, a new one pops up, so just wandering around the island racks up miles fast. 5,000 won't take long.

You can get a flimsy fishing rod and a flimsy net in the Resident Services tent. Don't sell the fish and bugs you catch just yet — show them to Tom Nook. Once you've brought him 5 different species, Blathers agrees to come to the island, and his tent goes up the next day. So catching lots of different fish and bugs on day one gets your museum going sooner.

TipYou can craft up to 10 of an item at once. When crafting at a workbench in your own home, you can use materials straight from your storage — no need to move them into your pockets first.

02Days 2–3: Museum, shop, and house

Blathers's Tent

Once Blathers's Tent is up, you can donate fish and bugs to him. Take any fossils you dig up to him for assessment first, donate the ones the museum doesn't have yet, and keep duplicates to sell. Around this time you'll also learn to craft a shovel and a vaulting pole — with the vaulting pole you can jump across rivers and explore the other side. Donate 15 items in total and the museum gets upgraded into a proper building.

After paying off the tent

Once your miles are paid off, Tom Nook offers to build you a house. This time it's Bells: a 98,000-Bell loan with no deadline and no interest. The house is ready the next day, so pay it off at your own pace.

Timmy will also ask for help opening a shop. He needs 30 each of wood, softwood, hardwood, and iron nuggets. You get wood by chopping trees with an axe — each tree gives 3 per day, so rotate between several trees. Iron nuggets come from hitting rocks. You don't have to hand everything over at once; turn in each material as soon as you have 30. After all four are in, Nook's Cranny opens the next day, with hours from 8 AM to 10 PM.

03Days 4–7: New residents, bridges, and 3 stars

From here on, progress depends on how much you play each day. Work through these roughly in order.

1. Set up homes for 3 new residents. Tom Nook asks you to pick plots for the newcomers' houses and place a few pieces of furniture he specifies on each one. Along the way he'll ask you to build your first bridge and teach you to craft a ladder, which lets you climb cliffs.

2. Resident Services upgrade. Once all 3 residents have moved in, the Resident Services tent is rebuilt as a proper building, and Isabelle starts working there. Talk to her to check your island rating.

3. Campsite. Tom Nook asks you to pick a spot for a campsite. Once it's built, visitors drop by to camp from time to time, and if you hit it off, you can invite them to move to your island.

4. Able Sisters. Mabel sets up a stall in the plaza every now and then. Once the museum, shop, and Resident Services building are all done and you've bought something from her on two separate visits, she decides to open a shop on her third visit.

The upgraded Resident Services, home to both Tom Nook and Isabelle

With all that done, the next step is a 3-star island rating. Isabelle will tell you what's lacking — usually flowers, trees, or furniture placed outdoors — so just fill in the gaps. At 3 stars, K.K. Slider comes to play a concert, and afterward you get the Island Designer app for terraforming.

NoteYour island rating counts trees and flowers, so don't go chopping trees and digging holes everywhere early on. Think before you act.

04Little things to do every day

Beyond the main story, a batch of things refreshes on your island every day. Grab them as you go and your materials and Bells will pile up much faster.

Star-shaped cracks in the ground hide fossils — 4 a day. Dig them up and have Blathers assess them.

Every rock on your island gives materials each day, and one of them gives Bells. Dig two holes behind yourself before hitting it so you don't get knocked back, and you can get all 8 hits in.

A message bottle washes up on the beach every day with a DIY recipe inside. Take a walk along the shore and you'll find it.

Chat with your villagers often. Your friendship grows over time, and they'll sometimes give you gifts. If you catch a villager busy crafting at home, they'll teach you a new recipe too.

Nook's Cranny stocks different items every day. Buy any furniture you haven't seen before — once you miss it, there's no telling when it'll show up again.

Because these happen every day, they're surprisingly easy to forget. Nook Circle has a Daily Checklist you can tick off as you go. For a full daily routine, see "Your daily island routine."

05Making Bells early on

You'll need Bells for your home loan and expansions. Early on, these are your main sources of income.

The easiest is selling fruit. Your native fruit sells for 100 Bells each, while fruit from other islands sells for 500. Bring a few back from a mystery island or a friend's island and plant them — once they grow, you've got a money tree.

Next up are turnips. Daisy Mae visits on Sunday mornings to sell them, and from Monday to Saturday the shop buys them at a new price every morning and afternoon. Prices keep changing, so sell when they're high. Turnips spoil by the following Sunday, so make sure to sell them before then.

Everything you get from fishing and bug catching can be sold too. Once you get the hang of it, try catching tarantulas and scorpions at night, or fishing for the coelacanth on rainy days — they all sell for a lot.

Remember to deposit spare Bells in the ATM. The ATM at Resident Services pays a little interest every month, which beats carrying them around in your pockets.

Not sure which day to sell? Enter the prices you see each day into Turnip Predict in Nook Circle, and it'll work out the likely pattern for the week.

Turnip Predict: enter your buy price and each day's prices to forecast the week's pattern

TipDon't blow all your miles once the tent is paid off. At the Nook Stop in Resident Services, 2,000 miles gets you a Nook Miles Ticket — fly to a mystery island to chop bamboo, bring back fruit, and stock up on materials. With luck, you might even meet a villager you'd like to invite.

TipFlimsy tools break after a while. If you have the materials, craft 10 at once to keep spares, and switch over once you learn recipes for sturdier tools.

06Version changes

Crafting 10 at once and using storage materials when crafting at home were both added in Ver. 3.0, so update your game to 3.0 first.
